The Evolution of Intersectionality

History: From Concept to Script

Coined by Kimberlé Crenshaw (1989), intersectionality began as a specific legal tool to describe how black women faced overlapping discrimination that neither "race" nor "gender" laws alone captured.

Over time, the concept has migrated from a descriptive tool into a prescriptive script. Critics argue that it now often functions as a hierarchy of grievances, encouraging individuals to see themselves primarily through their tally of "oppressions" rather than their unique capabilities.
