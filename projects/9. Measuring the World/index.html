What are we actually measuring?

Economists often use GDP (Gross Domestic Product) as the main yardstick for a country's success. It measures the total value of goods and services produced. However, GDP only tells part of the story. It doesn't show who gets the money, how much damage is done to the environment, or whether people are actually thriving.

🔑 Core Metrics to Know

  • GDP per capita: Total economic output divided by the population (an average).
  • Median Income: The "middle" income where half the people earn more and half earn less.
  • Gini Coefficient: A score from 0 to 1 representing how equally income is shared.
  • MPI (Multidimensional Poverty Index): Looks at health, school, and living standards.

The American Market vs. The World

The United States has a massive GDP. If you compare individual US states to other countries, the numbers are shocking. For example, California's economy is larger than that of Germany. However, metrics like "Social Progress" vary wildly.

Comparison: USA and Denmark

The US has a higher GDP per capita, but Denmark ranks higher in "Happiness" indices. Why?

  • Cost of Living: University debt and housing often offset high American raw income.
  • Safety Net: Higher taxes in Denmark pay for services Americans must buy from the market.

The Progress Debate

Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI)

Includes economic growth but subtracts costs like pollution and crime.

Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)

Counts how many people lack clean water, electricity, or primary schooling.
