📈 Numbers, Trust, and Certainty in Economics

Physics Envy, Mathiness, and the Limits of Mathematical Modelling

📺 Watch First: Economics and the Push for Physics-Level Precision

Continue below to explore the activities and questions

What You Should Know

In the human sciences, economists often use mathematical models to describe and predict economic behaviour. Mathematics can add clarity and precision to an argument by showing how variables relate. But unlike physics, economic systems involve human decision-making, changing social contexts, and messy real-world constraints. That makes it harder to achieve the same kind of certainty we associate with physical laws.

This raises a TOK issue: numbers can look precise and authoritative, but precision is not the same as truth or reliability. The way a model is built — its assumptions, what it leaves out, and how it is tested — affects whether we should trust its conclusions.

🔑 Key TOK Terms

  • Model: A simplified representation of reality used to explain or predict.
  • Assumption: A starting claim taken as true to make a model work.
  • Evidence: Data or reasons used to support a conclusion.
  • Interpretation: How we make meaning from evidence and results.
  • Certainty: How confident we are that a knowledge claim is true.
  • Trust: Whether we accept a claim as reliable enough to act on.
  • Scope: The limits of where a knowledge claim applies.

Two Critiques You Will Use

  • Physics envy: When economics tries to copy physics by using heavy maths to look more “scientific,” even when human behaviour makes that precision unrealistic.
  • Mathiness: When mathematical symbols and formal language are used in a way that hides weak assumptions or weak links to evidence.

B. Economics vs Natural Sciences

4. Physics often studies repeatable patterns under controlled conditions. Why is this form of certainty difficult in economics?

5. Many economic models assume rational decision-makers and equilibrium. Why does it matter that real people often behave differently?

C. TOK Reflection

6. How does reliance on mathematical models affect the trustworthiness of economic knowledge claims?

7. When economists use complex maths for policy decisions, what trade-offs arise between certainty and interpretability?
